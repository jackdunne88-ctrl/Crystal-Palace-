# Client Stories

A good story makes one finding real. It should be hopeful and inspiring, and honest about the reality of homelessness.

## Structure: Before, After, Bridge
1. **Before:** where the client was when they came to us. Describe circumstances, not character: "had been sleeping rough for two years", not "was a rough sleeper with nothing to offer."
2. **After:** where they are now. Use something concrete: a qualification, a job held past 13 weeks, a tenancy kept, a new routine, volunteering, reconnecting with family.
3. **Bridge:** what made the difference. Name the service and the approach (for example, one-to-one coaching, a City & Guilds course at a Putting Down Roots site, IPS employment support) and the client's own strengths and choices.

Keep it to 100 to 200 words in a report, two or three sentences in a summary or briefing.

## Asset-based language
- The client is the subject of the story. They achieved; we supported.
- Lead with strengths, skills and aspirations.
- Avoid pity, rescue language and labels.
- Use the client's own words where they have agreed, and only quote what they actually said.

## Anonymisation and consent
- Change the name and say so ("not their real name").
- Remove or generalise details that could identify someone: exact location, service address, employer name, unusual personal details.
- Only use stories where consent for this use has been confirmed. If unsure, ask before including it.
- Never invent, combine or embellish stories. If an illustrative example is needed, label it clearly as illustrative.

## Template
**[Name, not their real name]**

**Before:** [circumstances when they came to St Mungo's]

**After:** [where they are now, with a concrete outcome]

**Bridge:** [what supported the change, and what they brought to it]

[Optional: one short quote from the client, with consent]
