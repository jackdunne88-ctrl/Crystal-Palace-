# Narrative Frameworks

## Answer first (Pyramid Principle)
Lead with the answer, then three supporting arguments, then the evidence for each.

```
Governing thought (the message or ask)
├── Key line 1 → evidence
├── Key line 2 → evidence
└── Key line 3 → evidence
```

- The governing thought is one specific, declarative sentence. "Investing in Employment Support in [region] will move [X] more clients into work each year", not "This paper looks at employment outcomes."
- Three key lines is ideal. Keep them parallel: all reasons, or all outcomes, not a mix.
- Key lines should not overlap, and together they should fully support the governing thought.
- Stop at three levels. Detail goes in an appendix.

**Best for:** internal leadership, one-page briefings, emails asking for a decision.

## Situation, Complication, Resolution (SCR)
- **Situation:** the shared context the reader will agree with. Keep it short.
- **Complication:** what has changed, or the risk. This is why the reader should care.
- **Resolution:** what we recommend and why. This is most of the content.

Illustrative shape (placeholders, not real figures):
- Situation: Our services supported [X] clients into independent accommodation last year.
- Complication: Without ongoing support, some of those tenancies are at risk, and funding for [service] ends in [month].
- Resolution: Continued investment of [£X] would sustain progression support for [X] clients and protect the outcomes already achieved.

**Best for:** funders and commissioners who need context first; MPs and ministers where the policy problem must be established before the ask.

## Before, After, Bridge (BAB)
- **Before:** where the client or the service was.
- **After:** where they are now, with an outcome that can be measured or described.
- **Bridge:** what made the difference, meaning the service, the approach and the client's own strengths.

**Best for:** client stories, impact sections, showing the value of a specific programme. See `client-stories.md`.

## Spark, Problem, Bridge (SPB)
- **Spark:** a striking figure or contrast.
- **Problem:** why it matters for clients or funding.
- **Bridge:** what we do next.

**Best for:** short callouts, newsletters, social posts, a strong opening line.

## STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result)
**Best for:** looking back, such as end-of-grant reports, case studies and lessons learned.

## Choosing a framework

| Audience or situation | Framework |
|---|---|
| Internal leadership, time-limited | Answer first |
| Trust funder report or bid | SCR, with BAB for client stories |
| Government funder or commissioner | SCR, leading on outcomes, social value and value for money |
| MP or minister | SPB opening, then SCR, combining data, a client story and the economic case |
| Staff update or training | Answer first, kept plain and warm |
| End-of-grant or retrospective | STAR |

## Common failures
- **Burying the lead:** paragraphs of background before the point.
- **Findings without a "so what":** "We supported [X] clients" needs a comparison and a consequence.
- **Data dump:** tables with no interpretation.
- **No ask:** every piece ends with a decision, an action or a clear request.
