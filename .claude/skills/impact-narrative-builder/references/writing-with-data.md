# Writing With Data

## Audiences

| Audience | What they need | Lead with |
|---|---|---|
| Internal leadership | The ask and the support needed, fast | The ask, then two or three headline stats |
| Trust funders | Clear narrative behind the figures, real client voice | Outcomes and a client story |
| Government funders and commissioners | Outcomes, reach, collaboration, social value, value for money | Outcomes against targets, cost per outcome where known |
| MPs and ministers | A case for change | Data, a client story and the economic argument together |
| Internal staff | Clear, practical, warm | What it means for their work |

Assume leadership, funders in the sector and staff know IPS, trauma-informed and person-centred practice. For partners new to the sector, explain each in one plain sentence.

## Metrics to draw on
Clients accessing services; qualifications gained (City & Guilds, OCN); clients gaining employment; employment sustained at 13 weeks; digital devices provided; improved digital skills; independent living skills developed; clients accessing external courses and volunteering; client-reported improvements in wellbeing, confidence, self-esteem, social connections and skills.

## Numbers
- **Give every number context:** compared to last year, to target, or to a benchmark. "Up 12% on last year", not "12%".
- **Round for recall:** "around 200 clients" in a summary; exact figures in the data section.
- **No false precision.** One decimal place is usually enough for a rate.
- **Spell out a number that starts a sentence.**
- **Make it human:** "[X] clients are now in work and paying rent from their wages" lands harder than a percentage alone.
- **Show the link to sustained independence:** employment, skills and wellbeing are what help clients keep their homes.

## Sentences
- Active voice for findings: "Clients gained [X] OCN qualifications."
- One idea per sentence.
- State findings directly. Avoid "it appears that" and "it seems".

| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| "Significant impact" | The actual number and what changed |
| "Service users" | "Clients" |
| "Homeless people" | "People experiencing homelessness" |
| "Further work is needed" | "The next step is [action] by [owner] by [date]" |
| "We hope this is useful" | A clear ask or decision |

## Uncertainty
Have a view, and be honest about confidence.
- "Our best estimate is…"
- "This is based on [X] responses, so treat it as indicative."
- "This assumes [Y]. If [Y] changes, the figure becomes [Z]."

Never overstate a finding to make it more compelling. If a figure cannot be verified, leave it out and flag it.

## Charts
- **Title = the finding:** "Employment sustained at 13 weeks rose for the third year running", not "13-week sustainment by year".
- One annotation per chart, under ten words, on the point that matters.
- One chart on a one-page summary. Tables and detail go to the appendix.
